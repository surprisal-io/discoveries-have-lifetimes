Setting: discoveries with lifetimes

The relevant unit is not “H is true,” but “H describes the world during this specific time interval.”

Open-ended discovery systems search for unexpected structure, propose candidate hypotheses and gather evidence for them, instead of only answering questions or validating hypotheses supplied by a user. In adversarial environments, what they find can stop being true. In fraud, for example, attackers exploit a transaction pattern until it is detected or blocked, then change timing, account structure or money flow.

Existing systems implicitly treat a validated finding as permanent: once added to the set of discoveries, it is not revisited. We instead model a discovery as a claim about the current data-generating process, with an onset, an expiration and a lifetime in between. The framework has three parts: a generator proposes candidate regimes, an e-process accumulates evidence that a proposed regime is currently present, and an e-detector detects when it stops being present. The statistical tools are existing ones; the contribution is to treat discovery as a temporal process and connect these tools into one lifecycle.

Latent strategies with lifetimes. Each regime holds only on an interval, opening at τon and closing at τoff. A discovery H asserts that the current law Pt belongs to a family 𝒫H; it is correct only while the regime is active.

Why e-values: establishment and expiration

An e-variable is a non-negative random variable whose expectation under the null is at most one. Products of conditional e-values form e-processes, which remain valid under optional stopping. By Ville’s inequality, rejecting when an e-process crosses 1/α gives an anytime-valid test at level α. A temporal discovery involves two changes in the data-generating process, and each is handled by an existing e-value tool.

Establishment

is the regime present?

By the time the generator proposes H, the strategy may already have been active for some time, and the system did not know what to look for before then. We therefore do not try to locate the onset retrospectively. An e-process started after the proposal accumulates prospective evidence that H currently holds; the data used to propose H are not reused.

Mt = ∏s>tprop (1 + λs(Zs − pH))
H₀Pt ∉ 𝒫H  ·  establish H when Mt ≥ 1/α

Expiration

did the regime end?

Once H is established, the system knows what to monitor; only the time of departure is unknown. Null and alternative swap roles: the null is now that H still holds. An e-detector (Shin, Ramdas & Rinaldo, 2023) runs e-processes started at every possible change time and raises an alarm when the regime has likely ended.

Rt = (1 + Rt−1)(1 + γ(pH − Zt))
H₀Pt ∈ 𝒫H  ·  alarm when Rt ≥ A; average run length to a false alarm ≥ A

Anytime validity

Evidence can be checked after every observation, and no stopping rule has to be fixed in advance.

Existing constructions

For a one-sided constraint on the mean of a bounded statistic, standard betting constructions give test supermartingales for both roles. We do not introduce a new e-value.

No labels needed

In the fraud example the monitored claim concerns an observable predicate ZH, so monitoring does not require fraud labels.

Interactive demo: the lifecycle on a synthetic stream

Each simulated transaction either matches the predicate ZH = 1{amount ≥ 5000} or not. Legitimate traffic matches at about 1%. At times the system does not know, a simulated laundering ring becomes active and raises the match rate. The system sees only the stream: it estimates a baseline, proposes a candidate H when the recent match rate is unusually high, runs an e-process to establish that the rate is currently at least pH = 0.03, and then runs an e-detector to decide when this stops being true.

False establishment under the null

500 streams with match rate exactly pH = 0.03, the boundary of the null and its least favourable case. The e-process runs for 5,000 transactions and is checked after every one. Ville’s inequality bounds the probability of ever crossing 1/α by α. The observed fraction varies from run to run.

validity check · expiration

False expiration while the ring persists

200 streams in which the ring stays active at the current match rate. Each is first established, then the e-detector monitors 5,000 further transactions. The guarantee here is of a different kind: the average run length to a false alarm is at least A = 10⁴. This controls how often false alarms occur, not the probability of one within a fixed window.

Simulation details. The setup follows Appendix A of the paper on a smaller stream (16,000 transactions) with simulated rather than gen-fraud-graph data, so the numbers will differ from the paper’s run. Background match rate ≈ 0.01; actionability level pH = 0.03; baseline estimated on the first 3,000 transactions; a candidate is proposed when the trailing 500-transaction window exceeds the baseline by five binomial standard deviations; λs is a truncated Kelly plug-in; the e-detector is Shiryaev–Roberts with γ fixed from the baseline and A = 10⁴. Two conveniences are specific to the demo: a candidate not established within 4,000 transactions is dropped, and in automatic mode a new ring starts 1,600–2,500 transactions after each expiration alarm.

An autonomous scientist in an adversarial world must do more than discover what is true. It must know which of its discoveries still describe the world.
